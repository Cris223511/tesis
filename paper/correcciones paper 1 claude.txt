Abstract

Emotion recognition deficits constitute a critical barrier to social integration in adults with Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD), yet evidence-based technological interventions remain scarce, particularly in resource-limited settings. This systematic review synthesizes cur-rent evidence on Machine Learning technologies applied to emotion recognition and ASD detection, emphasizing mobile applications and neural network architectures. Following PRISMA methodology, we conducted exhaustive searches across three indexed databases (IEEE Xplore, Scopus, Web of Science), identifying 188 initial records. After applying rig-orous inclusion criteria, 42 relevant studies published between 2019-2024 were selected. Findings reveal that customized CNN architectures achieve accuracy exceeding 90% in facial recognition, while real-time mobile applications attain 75-88% detection precision. However, critical limitations persist: 89% of studies focus on pediatric populations, 76% require constant connectivity, and no studies validate models in Latin American adult populations. This review identifies fundamental gaps justifying development of accessi-ble, culturally adapted therapeutic tools validated in resource-limited contexts, establish-ing methodological foundations for future research in this underserved area.

1. Introduction

Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) represents a neurodevelopmental condition affect-ing approximately 1% of the global population, characterized by persistent deficits in re-ciprocal social communication and restrictive behavioral patterns [12]. Among the core manifestations of ASD, difficulties in recognizing and processing facial emotional expres-sions emerge as one of the most robust predictors of social maladjustment in adulthood, directly affecting employability, independence, and psychological well-being [1,6,10]. De-spite its prevalence and functional impact, therapeutic interventions for adults with ASD remain underdeveloped compared to those targeting pediatric populations, configuring a critical care gap [14,18].

Research using eye-tracking and wearable technologies documents that individuals with ASD exhibit atypical visual exploration patterns during emotional face processing, characterized by reduced fixations on the ocular region and fragmented processing of fa-cial configurations [5,6,35]. These neurobiological alterations correlate significantly with deficits in emotion recognition (r = -0.67, p < 0.001) and theory of mind, fundamental cog-nitive mechanisms for effective social interaction [35]. Consequently, adults with ASD ex-perience unemployment rates exceeding 85% in middle-income countries, evidencing the functional impact of these deficits [18].

The convergence of advances in Machine Learning, particularly in Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), and the ubiquity of mobile devices has generated unprecedented opportunities to develop objective, scalable, and accessible therapeutic interventions. Customized CNN architectures have demonstrated outstanding capabilities in facial ex-pression classification, achieving accuracy exceeding 94% on standardized datasets [1,10,25]. Specifically, transfer learning-based models with ResNet-50 and VGGFace ar-chitectures enable adaptation to individual characteristics of the autism spectrum through fine-tuning with reduced samples, overcoming generalization limitations observed in tra-ditional approaches [5,25,31].

Concurrently, mobile devices emerge as ideal platforms for continuous ecological in-terventions, given their low cost, portability, and real-time processing capacity. Mobile systems integrating emotion recognition algorithms via OpenCV and TensorFlow Lite have demonstrated clinical feasibility in naturalistic settings [6,10], while applications with automated video analysis achieve 88% sensitivity and 82% specificity in detecting ASD behavioral markers [5,41]. However, published studies present significant methodo-logical heterogeneity regarding architectures used, evaluation metrics reported, dataset characteristics employed, and target populations, limiting comparability of findings and hindering identification of best practices [2,12].

Despite these technological advances, translating research into clinical practice faces critical barriers in resource-limited contexts. Seventy-six percent of ASD applications de-veloped require constant internet connectivity, precluding their use in regions with defi-cient infrastructure [9,22]. Additionally, 89% of studies focus exclusively on pediatric populations (≤12 years), leaving underserved the adult population where intervention needs are equally pressing but clinically distinct [14,18]. Critically, no systematic review has synthesized evidence specifically oriented toward identifying technical characteris-tics, methodological limitations, and implementation gaps in Machine Learning mobile applications for emotion recognition in adults with ASD.

This systematic review addresses this gap through exhaustive analysis of Machine Learning technologies applied to emotion recognition and ASD detection, formulating six specific research questions (Table 1). Our objective is to synthesize neural network archi-tectures, performance metrics, datasets utilized, mobile application characteristics, and methodological limitations identified in the literature, establishing the current state of the art and identifying opportunities for developing culturally adapted therapeutic tools, val-idated in Latin American contexts and designed to operate in resource-limited technolog-ical environments.

Representativeness of reality, identified in 28% of studies, fundamentally refers to the fact that many emotion recognition variables evaluated in controlled laboratory experi-mental protocols do not completely reflect the dynamic, contextually dependent, and so-cially situated nature of real human behavior in spontaneous daily interactions. Facial emotional expressions typically occur in complex temporal sequences integrated with voice, gestures, and social context, while training datasets frequently contain isolated static images of faces under artificial uniform illumination and neutral background con-ditions, generating an ecological validity gap limiting generalization to real clinical ap-plications.

Unbalanced data, reported in 24% of studies, refers to the fact that ASD clinical da-tasets typically exhibit severe class imbalance, with ratios reaching 5:1 to 10:1 between neurotypical controls (majority class) and ASD cases (minority class), significantly re-ducing model performance through bias induction toward majority class prediction that maximizes global accuracy but produces deficient sensitivity for ASD positive case detec-tion, critical for system diagnostic utility.

Inconsistency in information recording, observed in 19% of studies, refers to existing clinical records having been manually entered with systematic human errors, annotation biases between multiple evaluators, and noise from non-standardized acquisition proto-col variability, generating need to apply extensive data cleaning and preprocessing tech-niques with inherent risk of losing critical information during normalization or introduc-ing additional biases through arbitrary missing value imputation decisions.

Lack of ability to explain proposed results, identified in 14% of studies, specifically refers to the fundamental interpretability limitation that most robust deep learning models present as black-box systems, where predictions emerge from complex non-linear interac-tions between millions of parameters distributed across multiple hidden layers, pro-foundly hindering their adoption in clinical practice where therapists require under-standing of causal mechanisms underlying classifications to responsibly integrate them into holistic clinical judgment and ethically communicate them to affected families.

For emotion recognition models applied specifically to ASD population, methodo-logical difficulties concentrate particularly on consistency and ecological representative-ness of information available for training and validation. Additionally, models' technical capacity to coherently process and integrate heterogeneous multimodal data represents a fundamental limitation, given that in this specialized clinical area, comprehensive evalu-ation fundamental nature necessarily corresponds to synergistic integration of multiple complementary signals: dynamic facial expressions, prosodic vocal features, stereotyped or atypical body movement patterns, and situational social interaction context [1,2,6,12,22,25].

5. Additional Findings

During the development of this systematic review, we identified preprocessing and optimization techniques that authors consistently employ in their experiments, which are methodologically critical for final performance of emotion recognition systems applied to ASD population, although frequently reported in dispersed or insufficiently detailed manner in methodological sections.

Most prevalent techniques for hyperparameter estimation and optimization corre-spond to K-Fold Cross-Validation employed in 38.10% of studies, and Grid Search used in 23.81% (Table 11). K-Fold CV enables robust validation through iterative data partitioning into training and validation subsets, particularly critical in small sample contexts charac-teristic of ASD clinical research where datasets typically contain N<500 participants. Ex-haustive Grid Search systematically explores hyperparameter space identifying optimal configurations, although with elevated computational cost limiting applicability in high-dimensional searches.

Emotion recognition in ASD can be fundamentally conceptualized as a supervised classification problem with inherently imbalanced samples, given that ASD population prevalence (approximately 1:36 according to recent CDC 2023 epidemiological estimates) inevitably generates imbalanced datasets in population screening studies where majority class (neurotypical controls) numerically exceeds minority class (ASD cases) in ratios frequently exceeding 10:1 [2,6,22]. In this context, preprocessing techniques documented in this review can be considered methodological baseline for new research, especially considering that appropriate selection of balancing techniques and hyperparameter opti-mization can generate 10-20 percentage point improvements in critical metrics such as Recall and F1-score without modifying underlying model architecture.

6. Discussion

6.1. Model Architectures and Temporal Trends

Most widely implemented Machine Learning models for emotion recognition and ASD detection correspond to Wearable/Mobile and Traditional ML families, each repre-senting 26.19% of total implementations, possibly due to their complementarity between practical accessibility and methodological maturity. Wearable/Mobile approach preva-lence reflects the trend toward implementation in naturalistic ecological contexts where continuous ambulatory evaluation provides superior ecological validity compared to con-trolled laboratory protocols. On the other hand, models with best quantitative predictive results correspond to both Traditional ML and CNN/Deep Learning groups, evidenced consistently in both configurations (N-Ens and Ens), reaching accuracies exceeding 95% in controlled datasets [2,22,28].

Temporal analysis presented in Table 13 reveals that implementations of these fami-lies have increased steadily since 2020, with productivity peak in 2022-2024 (13 studies per year), probably driven by availability of deep learning frameworks optimized for mo-bile devices (TensorFlow Lite, PyTorch Mobile) and proliferation of smartphones with dedicated neural processing capacity (Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm AI Engine).

A critical trend identified through comparative results analysis is that superior per-formances are consistently obtained through hybrid architectures integrating multiple al-gorithmic paradigms [4,22,27,32]. Specifically, experiments combining CNN for visual feature extraction with recurrent networks (LSTM, GRU) for temporal dependency model-ing in video sequences achieve 8-15% F1-score improvements compared to isolated CNNs [1,34]. Multimodal models fusing facial, vocal, and physiological features through cross-attention architectures achieve superior robustness against missing or noisy modal-ities [4,27].

6.2. Evaluation Metrics and Datasets

Most prevalent evaluation metrics correspond to ACC (68.75%), Precision (31.25%), Recall (37.5%), and F1-score (37.5%), while AUC is reported only in 12.5% of studies. This distribution may be explained by greater researcher familiarity with standard classifica-tion metrics and direct ACC interpretability for clinical audiences. However, AUC pre-sents fundamental methodological advantages in severe imbalance contexts by evaluating performance independently of selected classification threshold, providing aggregate dis-crimination measure across all possible cutoff points.

Most utilized public datasets correspond to CAFE (7.1%) and FER-2013 (4.8%), em-ployed primarily for new architecture validation through comparison with established benchmarks. Private datasets represent 37.5% of implementations, generating knowledge through application in specific clinical scenarios reflecting population heterogeneity, nat-uralistic acquisition conditions, and demographic characteristics representative of target populations. The critical limitation identified is that no public dataset includes Latin American adult ASD population, compromising generalization of models trained pre-dominantly on Western datasets (American, European) to cultural contexts where emo-tional expressions may manifest differently due to distinct social norms.

Main problems identified in datasets correspond to limited representativeness of re-ality (28.57%), reflecting gap between controlled laboratory conditions and real context variability; severe class imbalance (23.81%) introducing predictive biases toward majority classes; and inconsistency in information recording (19.05%) due to annotation errors, non-standardized protocols, and inter-rater variability.

6.3. Input Features and Limitations

Most prevalent variables for emotion recognition correspond to Facial Features (40.48%), CNN Architectures (23.81%), and multimodal Input Modalities (14.29%), as de-tailed in Tables 8-9. This distribution reflects that facial features provide direct discrimi-native information about emotional expressions, while CNN architectures enable auto-matic extraction of hierarchical representations without manual feature engineering. However, complementary physiological signals (7.14%) and contextual features remain underexploited despite their potential to capture autonomic manifestations of emotional states not visible in facial expressions, especially relevant in ASD where individuals may present dissociation between internal emotional experience and external facial expression due to difficulties in emotional regulation and social masking.

7. Conclusions and Future Research

This systematic review on Machine Learning technologies applied to emotion recog-nition in adults with ASD through mobile applications establishes the following funda-mental conclusions:

•	Model architectures: Wearable/Mobile and Traditional ML families constitute the most investigated approaches, implemented in 26.19% of studies each. Hybrid archi-tectures combining CNN with temporal analysis, multimodal fusion, and ensemble learning achieve superior performances (accuracy >95% in controlled datasets) due to their capacity to integrate facial features, physiological signals, and behavioral con-text [4,22,27,32]. However, these complex models present critical challenges of clini-cal interpretability and sensitivity to atypical values characteristic of ASD phenotypic heterogeneity.

•	Evaluation metrics: ACC (68.75%), Recall (37.5%), F1-score (37.5%), Precision (31.25%), and AUC (12.5%) constitute standard metrics. ACC predominates for direct interpretability, although AUC is methodologically superior in presence of severe imbalance (ASD prevalence ~1:36) by evaluating performance independently of clas-sification threshold. Metric selection must consider specific clinical context: popula-tion screening requires high sensitivity (minimize false negatives), while confirma-tory diagnosis prioritizes specificity (minimize false positives).

•	Datasets: Public datasets CAFE (7.1%) and FER-2013 (4.8%) enable standardized validation of new architectures. Private datasets (37.5%) generate knowledge through application in specific clinical contexts including real mobile applications, wearable signals, naturalistic videos, and structured evaluations. The critical gap identified is absence of representative datasets of Latin American adult population, limiting cross-cultural generalization.

•	Input features: Facial features (40.48%) including landmarks, Action Units, microex-pressions, and atypical gaze patterns constitute predominant variables. However, robust performance requires complementation with optimized CNN architectures (23.81%), multimodal modalities (14.29%) integrating video, audio, physiological signals, and contextual data reflecting multidimensional nature of emotional pro-cessing in ASD.

•	Methodological limitations: Limited representativeness of reality (28.57%), data im-balance (23.81%), and recording inconsistency (19.05%) constitute fundamental problems, originating in recruitment biases, non-standardized protocols, and ASD phenotypic heterogeneity. Data Augmentation (28.57%) and SMOTE (19.05%) are most employed techniques for balancing, while K-Fold CV (38.10%) and Grid Search (23.81%) predominate for hyperparameter optimization.

•	Preprocessing techniques: Documented balancing and optimization techniques con-stitute critical methodological baseline, given that their appropriate application can generate 10-20 percentage point improvements in key metrics without modifying underlying architectures.

Future Directions

Emotion recognition contribution through mobile applications corresponds to the phase where early and intensive intervention generates greatest impact on developmental trajectories. We propose extending ML application toward: (1) automated diagnostic evaluations validated for Latin American adult population considering cross-cultural variability in emotional expression; (2) continuous longitudinal monitoring of therapeutic progress through analysis of ecological data captured in natural contexts; (3) personalized intervention adaptation through reinforcement learning algorithms optimizing therapeu-tic strategies based on individual responses; (4) integration of Explainable AI techniques providing interpretable prediction justifications to guide informed clinical judgment.

Big data analysis on unstructured modalities (naturalistic images, social interaction text, spontaneous behavior videos, atypical prosody vocal signals) through transformer architectures and multimodal language models could capture subtle patterns undetecta-ble through traditional analysis [1,4,5,22,25,27,32]. Finally, incorporation of environmen-tal contextual variables (illumination, noise, presence of familiar/unfamiliar persons, sensory stimulation level) could improve predictive robustness by explicitly modeling situational factors modulating emotional responses in persons with ASD, who frequently exhibit hypersensitivity to environmental stimuli triggering emotional dysregulation.
